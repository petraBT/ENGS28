Activity 13.2.11. Connect and power the servo.

Do this once your pulse is verified on the oscilloscope, and work through it in order.
🔗

(a)

With nothing powered, remove the AD2 from PA7, plug the regulator board into the breadboard, and run a wire from the row of its GND pin to the breadboard’s ground rail, the rail the Nucleo’s GND already feeds. Leave the row of its 5 V pin empty for now.
🔗
🔗

(b)

Plug the power adapter into the regulator board, and nothing else. With the multimeter on DC volts, the black probe on the breadboard’s ground rail and the red probe in the row of the regulator board’s 5 V pin, you should read 5 V. We measure before the servo is connected, and we do not want to connect the servo until the 5 V is confirmed. A reading of 0 V means nothing is reaching the board, so check the adapter and how it is seated. A reading near the adapter’s own voltage means the 5 V and Vin rows are swapped or the regulator has failed. Then unplug the adapter.
🔗
🔗

(c)

With nothing powered, connect the servo’s three leads: the brown lead to the breadboard’s ground rail, the red center lead into the row of the regulator board’s 5 V pin, not its GND or Vin pin, and the orange signal lead to D11 (PA7). Then check your wiring with an instructor.
🔗
🔗

(d)

Plug in the USB cable first, then the power adapter into the regulator board. Turn the knob, and you should see the servo rotate, smoothly, as you adjust the potentiometer.
🔗
🔗
